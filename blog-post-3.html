As We May Think by Vannevar Bush, 1945

September 22, 2026

Reading Vannevar Bush’s “As We May Think” honestly made me feel a little sad because of how hopeful he seemed about the future of knowledge. He describes scientists as having “worked in effective partnership” (p.112) during the war and imagines what could happen if that same collaboration continued afterward. He recognizes that there is an overwhelming amount of information, but his solution is to build better ways for people to find and connect it. His idea of the memex especially stood out to me because he says, “The human mind does not work that way. It operates by association.” He imagines a system where people could build “trails of thought” between different pieces of information and preserve those connections for others. There is something really hopeful about the idea that knowledge is supposed to become more accessible, shared, and interconnected. The poor image, as we spoke about last week, has a very different relationship to information. The poor image is degraded because it has been copied, compressed, reposted, pirated, or pulled away from its original context, but it continues to circulate. It can preserve access to something even when the original is restricted or unavailable. This makes me think about how much information is suppressed today by companies, governments, etc. Information does not always have to be completely destroyed in order to be controlled. YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, these platforms contain algorithms that will purposely obscure content from reaching larger audiences. Sometimes the content is harmful, sure, but sometimes the content simply doesn’t align with the narrative that supports these corporations. Hidden, restricted, difficult to access knowledge is still being controlled, now by AI systems that determine what is considered valuable knowledge in the first place. That’s really why this essay felt so strange to read, because his optimism is simultaneously familiar and far away from our current reality.
