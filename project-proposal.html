Abstract:

As shadows are proof of the presence of light, deterioration is proof of the presence of life. My photographs will document traces of wear, decay, and human interaction/design, which I will use as the foundation for digital illustrations incorporating texture, text, and imagery. By suggesting human intervention without depicting the human form, I aim to explore simply how it feels to miss something or someone.

Project Description:

For this semester-long project, I hope to manipulate and collage images that evoke feelings of emptiness and longing while simultaneously acknowledging and implying what must have been there before. In the first phase, I photographed spaces and shadows that looked almost like uncanny boundaries. I was very interested in capturing images that had confusing proportions or perspectives, images that make the viewer question where we are, what angle is this from, how high off of the ground are we? But I also aimed to capture images that feel touched by people, that look as though there should be a human subject or part of a human subject.

Again, for phase one I only explored around Harlem but I would like to continue photographing other personal, familial spaces as well. Not necessarily inside of my family’s homes, rather places people have met or gotten lost, places they love or have felt love in. Part of this project for me, and the importance of the “incantation” theme is the tradition and ritual that I would like to develop. So often I shy away from visiting places I feel so connected to, even when the people who connected me to them are still alive. The repetition and process of capturing these spaces, with memories that I could almost visualize, memories of people who were in my life, it forces me to direct energy to the past and bring it here into the present, which I feel is magical. I don’t want to only photograph spaces but objects as well, imprinted furniture, worn out jewelry, faded decorations. I have thoughts on inserting myself into these images somehow, maybe through digital drawings. The photographs I take will be collaged and edited, then printed and written on.

The poems act as “spells” of sorts, manipulation of words representing a manipulation of energy. The poems will be handwritten, perhaps across several images (an unknown amount of images). I’d like to compile all of these pages and bind them into a book of spells. Something I can read and look at, moments and memories I can put myself into as I read them, hopefully filling in some of the empty space where new memories would go if given the chance. The book binding itself becomes part of the ritual act, like an artifact of nonexistent moments in time.

Ultimately, I’m interested in the relationship between absence and presence, and how photographs and objects and words can make something absent feel temporarily present again.
