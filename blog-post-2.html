The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction by Walter Benjamin, 1935

September 15, 2026

The question I asked myself, which framed the reading for me, was ‘what is capitalism doing to our art and where is that leading us?’. In the second section, Benjamin states “reproduction of a work of art is lacking in … its presence in time and space”, which tells me that authenticity must only exist outside of mechanised production. The original making and experience of a work of art is composed of all its context in relation to just that: time and space. So, an original work is the object (the sculpture, the painting, the actors in the play), the history, and the location of making. Benjamin refers to this combination of things as “aura”.

With film, the image is separated from spacetime, put into its own world that can travel anywhere, be manipulated by anyone, for any reason. This removes the images from their location and historical context, which weakens its aura. But, and this goes back to my original question, a “weak aura” seems to be exactly what capitalism does and needs from art. Strong aura in an artwork is representational of a strong message, stance, a true history, a real thing that happened because of other real things that also happened; while capitalism’s transformation of art into a production system allows those who control the production and distribution of images to have more control over the context surrounding them. If aura is connected to an artwork’s history, location, and presence, then removing an image from that context also creates an opportunity to control what parts of that history remain visible.
